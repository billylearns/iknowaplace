Why this exists

During my time backpacking through Southeast Asia, I received many recommendations from friends and family. They were scattered across texts, Instagram DMs, Google Docs, my Notes app and probably a few other places I've already forgotten about. The recommendations are valuable, but unorganized. Also, there wasn't a consistent way to see which ones fit the time, budget and energy I had on a particular day. I wanted to see what I could do about it while learning how to vibe code something from scratch in the process.

I Know a Place brings those recommendations together. Each entry includes what someone thought, how they rated it and approximately how much time, money and energy it requires. When you'd rather not piece together the day yourself, Plan my trip uses those recommendations to suggest a few options that fits what you feel like doing.

This is intended to feel different than other review/recommendation sites like TripAdvisor or Google Maps. The value comes from the context behind each recommendation. Opinions from friends and family whose tastes you understand, whose judgment you trust and who have a sense of what you might actually enjoy, unlike strangers on the internet. AI is useful to help sort and organize the options, but it doesn't invent the recommendations or replace the opinions behind them.

Toronto was the starting point for this project, a way to test the idea using genuine recommendations for a city I know. Now that I've seen what it can do, I plan to use it for future trips by collecting recommendations from friends before I leave and turning them into something I can actually use once I arrive.

Hopefully I can spend less time digging through my phone and more time actually going places worth recommending.

— Billy Liu
